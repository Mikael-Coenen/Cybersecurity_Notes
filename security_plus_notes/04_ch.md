## Social Engineering
- Social engineering: the practice of manipulating people through a variety of strategies to accomplish desired actions

## Social Engineering Principles
- Principles leveraged to successfully social engineer an individual:
    - Authority: relies on the fact that most people will obey someone who appears to be in charge or knowledgeable, ex: someone posing as a manager
    - Intimidation: relies on scaring or bullying an individual into taking a desired action
    - Consensus-based social engineering: relies on the fact that people tend to want to do what others are doing, ex: everyone else in a department already clicked on a link
    - Scarcity: makes something look desirable because it may be the last one available
    - Familiarity-based attacks: relies on you liking the individual or organization the individual is claiming to represent
    - Trust: relies on a connection with the individual they are targeting
    - Urgency: relies on creating a feeling that the action must be taken quickly due to some reason

  ## Social Engineering Techniques
  - Phishing:
      - The fraudulent acquisition of info, often focused on credentials as well as sensitive personal info
      - Most often done via email but can be done various other ways (smishing, vishing)
      - Spear phishing: targets specific individuals or groups in an organization to try to get desired info or access
      - Whaling: like spear phishing but targets senior employees like CEOs and CFOs
      - One of the most common defenses against all types of phishing is awareness
      - Technical defenses also exist such as filtering
  - Vishing:
      - Phishing via voice or voicemail messages
      - Vishing aims to get targets to disclose personal, financial, or other useful info, or to send funds
      - Vishing often relies on a sense of urgency and often presents themselves as authorities
  - Smishing:
      - Phishing via text messages
      - Smishing aims to get users to click on a link in a text message
      - Smishing tries to build trust or urgency or establish authority
   
## Misinformation and Disinformation
- Online influence campaigns have become common and more used by governments and others as part of misinformation and disinformation campaigns
- Misinformation: incorrect info, often resulting from getting facts wrong
- Disinformation: incorrect, inaccurate, or outright false info that is intentionally provided
- MDM: misinformation, disinformation, malinformation
- CISA recommends a five-step TRUST process to counter misinformation and disinformation campaigns:
    - Tell your story
    - Ready your team
    - Understand and assess MDM
    - Strategize response
    - Track outcomes
